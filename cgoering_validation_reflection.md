# MiniProject3 - Validation Reflection

Davyn and I compared 100 labels (5 fields x 10 projects each way). We agreed on 66 before discussion and disagreed on 34, which we resolved over text with an agreed adjudicated value for every one. Agreement was highest on Currentstatus (90%) and ActivityPattern (85%) and lowest on RecentThemes (35%) and WhyRecovered (40%). The categorical labels held up because they follow from numbers (dates, yearly commit counts). The free-text ones depend on how closely you read commit messages, and on whether the writer finished the job: all ten of my WhyRecovered values were auto-generated placeholders I never edited, which alone accounts for 10 of the 34 disagreements.

**What came before long gaps.** The adjudicated labels show a few warning signs. Several projects were tied to a paper or one-time artifact and went quiet when it was done: ashiklom_edr-da's last real commits are manuscript revisions ("Address round 2 comments", "Update acknowledgments", April 2021), and exascaleinfolab_vadetis's last commits before its gap are mostly README edits plus a deployment script change. Others tapered off first. pseudomanifold_aleph fell from 1,349 commits in 2017 to 0-2 a year after 2022, and svalorzen_ai-toolbox's pre-gap commits were Docker and CI plumbing rather than library work. A shrinking commit count, or a tail of only documentation or infrastructure commits, was a better signal than the gap itself.

**What triggered recovery.** Where we agreed a project really recovered, there was a concrete cause. oxfordska_oskar resumed after lost repository data was restored from a failed Subversion server. paulhancock_aegean restarted when a new contributor started the BANE work. ctlab_fgsea came back around a hosting move and Bioconductor release bumps. gboeing_pynamical and mjwoods_rnetcdf resumed around versioned releases (v0.3.3; RNetCDF 1.0-3), and pnnl_isicle with a v2.0 refactor. trichter_rf's core developer returned with a burst of fixes. In most cases the same person or an outside contributor came back around a release or a specific need.

**When a gap does not mean the project is dead.** rnetcdf had a 39-month gap and then over 1,300 more commits; pynamical had a 34-month gap and resumed with release work. A gap by itself says little. What matters is what the project looks like on the other side. The opposite is also true: a single commit after a gap is not a recovery. ashiklom (a LICENSE file after 49 months), aleph (a PKGBUILD URL fix after 29), vadetis (two README edits after 41) and ai-toolbox (CI fixes and merges) were all labeled `No` after we agreed on one standard for both sets of projects.

**Recurring pitfalls and how we resolved them.** (1) Treating any new commit as recovery: resolved by requiring sustained development. (2) Counting automatic "Merge <sha> into <sha>" commits and bot builds (Documenter.jl) as features: resolved by reading the actual last 10 messages and labeling those `Other` or `Automated bot contributions`. (3) Keyword matching for themes: my script labeled whichever keyword it saw first, so "update" became feature work. (4) Applying the Currentstatus cutoff: two projects were marked Inactive even though their last commits were in 2026, after the 2025-04-01 cutoff. (5) Borderline patterns, e.g. steady vs. irregular for projects with commits every year but large swings, which we settled by looking at yearly counts against the coding guide's definitions. Writing the criteria down, and checking labels against specific commits, did more than picking the "right" label.
